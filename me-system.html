Context

I designed this M&E system for GDC's Paka-Baringo Silali project, a live 70 to 100MW geothermal steam field development in Kenya's Rift Valley. The system had to answer to three audiences at once: KfW and GRMF as financing partners, GDC's own project managers making weekly decisions, and the communities living inside the project footprint.

What follows is a condensed view built for this portfolio. The complete document runs to 19 indicators, four data collection tools, a full RACI matrix, a three-stage evaluation plan, and twelve annexes.

Indicator Reference Sheet

One indicator, in full

All 19 indicators carry a sheet like this one, so that anyone reviewing a number can see where it came from and how it was worked out. The three rows marked in red are the ones that decide whether the number holds up.

Impact IM.2 — Annual CO2 equivalent emissions avoided
Definition
Tonnes of CO2 equivalent avoided each year as Paka geothermal generation displaces fossil-fuel thermal generation on the national grid.
Calculation
GWh generated × 1,000 × 0.5 tCO2/MWh, using EPRA's published grid emission factor.
Baseline and target
0 tCO2e, plant not yet operational, rising to 277,000–394,200 tCO2e a year at 70 to 100MW and 90% capacity factor.
Data source
AMEA Power generation records via indicator MO.2, and EPRA's published grid emission factor.
Frequency and owner
Collected quarterly, reported annually once the plant runs. GDC M&E Division.
Who uses it
KfW climate finance reporting, GRMF, NEMA, Kenya's NDC reporting to the UNFCCC, and the GDC Board.
SDG crosswalk
SDG 13.2.2, greenhouse gas emissions per year. SDG 7.2.1, renewable energy share.
Disaggregation
By project phase, 70MW against 100MW, and annual against cumulative.
Contribution or attribution
Attribution. Emissions avoided come only from Paka plant generation, worked out with a standard international method. Most impact indicators on this project do not clear that bar and are written as contribution instead.
Data quality risk
EPRA revises the grid emission factor from time to time, which breaks comparability between years. The capacity factor assumption may also drift from what the plant actually does.
Mitigation
Use the latest published factor, record which factor was used in each annual report, and restate earlier years whenever it changes.
Running the whole system costs about USD 264,000 a year, roughly 0.13% of the project budget. That covers two full-time M&E officers, the community survey, safeguards monitoring, the annual data quality assessment, field visits and the Tableau licence.
Data Collection Tools

Four instruments, and who they ask

These four produce the evidence behind every indicator in the matrix. Two of them go to households inside the project area.

Tool 1 · Bi-annual

Community Perception and Welfare Survey

Household attitudes, satisfaction with benefit-sharing, changes in livelihood, water access and employment. Seven sections run from household profile through water, infrastructure, employment, community relations and grievances, to what people expect next.

56 questions · minimum 200 households
Stratified by location, gender and livelihood, within 15km of the field
Face to face in Pokot, Tugen or Swahili · 35 to 45 minutes
Tool 2 · Monthly

Environmental and Social Safeguards Checklist

Compliance against the Environmental and Social Commitment Plan and KfW's safeguards requirements, covering air and noise, water and groundwater, land and soil, biodiversity, occupational health and safety, community health and security, and social and economic safeguards.

47 items across 8 safeguard domains
Run by GDC's Environmental Officer with an external consultant
Feeds the grievance and community welfare indicators
Tool 3 · Monthly

Project Performance Tracking Template

Drilling progress, infrastructure milestones, financial utilisation and commercial milestone status. Seven sections, filled in by section heads and the AMEA Power project team, due by the 10th of the following month.

Feeds OP.1 to OP.7, IN.1 to IN.3 and the immediate outcome milestones
Carries risk and assumption status, issues and actions
Tool 4 · Bi-annual

Key Informant Interview Guide

Depth on outcomes, commercial progress, assumption status and how stakeholders read the project. Four informant groups spanning AMEA Power, EPRA, KfW, KPLC, KETRACO, Ministry officials and community leaders.

Seven sections · 45 to 60 minutes
Carries the qualitative half of the mixed-methods design

CHECK 01

CHECK 02

Validity

Trend consistency

Movement against earlier periods can be explained. Unexplained jumps go back to source.

The rule that does most of the work is the third one. Nothing significant is written up as a conclusion until a second, independent source says the same thing. Around it sit the ordinary safeguards: survey instruments are pre-tested with 10 to 15 households before each round, every enumerator sits a half-day training first, and a formal Data Quality Assessment runs each year on the five priority indicators.

Evaluation

Three evaluations, and what each can claim

Monitoring tells you whether the project is doing what it said it would. Evaluation asks whether it made a difference, and why. The six OECD-DAC criteria, written as questions about this project.

Relevance
Is the project solving the right problem, and does it still fit Kenya's energy policy as that policy moves?
Effectiveness
Are the intended outcomes arriving: commercial agreements, plant construction, power on the grid?
Efficiency
Are results being achieved at a sensible cost and inside planned timeframes?
Impact
Is the project shifting electricity costs, emissions, energy security and community welfare?
Sustainability
Will the results hold after the project ends, and is the commercial model viable long-term?
Coherence
Does it fit with KETRACO transmission, KPLC distribution and national electrification targets?

How the impact evaluation would actually work

The lead method is contribution analysis. It builds the causal argument one link at a time and asks what evidence exists at each step: GDC drills and confirms wells, steam capacity is confirmed at a commercially viable cost, AMEA Power commissions the plant, electricity is generated at 6.5 US cents per kWh, thermal generation is displaced, costs come down, CO2 is avoided. Where the evidence for a link is thin, the claim is written down as thin.

Difference-in-differences handles the community welfare side, comparing Baringo county indicators before and after commissioning against comparable counties, using KNBS household survey data alongside the project's own bi-annual community surveys.

Both methods are worked through in full, with the alternatives that were considered and the reasons they were set aside, in Document 03 →

Adaptive Management

What forces a decision

Three conditions require a written response to KfW, whatever the reporting calendar happens to say.

Cumulative well success rate falls below 80%

Triggers a reservoir engineering review and a revised drilling plan. Assumption AS.2, which the whole steam field rests on, puts the success rate at 90%.

Financial Investment Decision not reached within six months of PPA ratification

Triggers a commercial review between GDC, AMEA Power and KfW. Confirmed steam capacity is worth nothing until the plant that uses it is financed.

More than five community grievances unresolved past the agreed timeframe

Triggers a community relations review and a revision of the stakeholder engagement plan. Tool 2 counts the grievances and Tool 1 checks them independently.

Underneath the triggers is the ordinary rhythm. The Project Manager convenes a management review on the first working day of each month, with the dashboard and the monthly report as the agenda. Twice a year a longer learning review asks what is working better than expected and why, what is not, what should change in the next six months, and what of it applies to GDC's other geothermal fields.
